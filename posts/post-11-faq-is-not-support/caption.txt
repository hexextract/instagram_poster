Your FAQ page isn't customer support. It's a filing cabinet you're asking customers to search.

Every FAQ page is built on the same assumption: that people who have a question will go and look for the answer, find it, recognise it, and believe it.

In practice, someone messages you for one of three reasons.

They did not find it. Your FAQ has nineteen entries and theirs is number fourteen.

They did not trust what they found. Generic policy language reads as something written by a lawyer to protect you, not by you to help them.

Their question has a detail yours does not. "Can I return it" is answered. "Can I return it if I have worn it once outdoors and the sole came away" is not, and that is the actual question.

An FAQ deflects the easy twenty percent. That is genuinely useful. The problem is that it lets you believe you have handled support, when what you have handled is the part that was never difficult.

Be honest: when did you last update yours?
