Here's the ROI maths without the invented percentages.

Every number below is one you can replace with your own. That is the entire point of it.

Step 1. Count your repetitive tickets. Use the audit from post 28 if you ran it, otherwise count one normal week. Say 210 tickets, of which roughly 70% are the same eight questions. About 147.

Step 2. Multiply by minutes. Ninety seconds is fair for reading a question, checking one thing and writing a proper reply. Use two minutes if you are being honest about context switching. 147 by 1.5 minutes is about 3.7 hours a week.

Step 3. Price the founder hours. 3.7 hours across 13 weeks is about 48 hours a quarter. Six working days, spent on eight questions you have already answered a hundred times.

Step 4. The other side. About 1,911 repetitive queries a quarter at about twelve cents a resolved query is about $229.

So: 48 hours against roughly $229.

Whether that trade is good depends entirely on what your 48 hours are worth, and only you know that.

What this maths deliberately ignores, because we cannot prove it: sales saved by answering at 2am, baskets kept by catching one doubt at checkout, markets opened by answering in 50+ languages. All real, none counted here. The case above is made on repetition alone.
