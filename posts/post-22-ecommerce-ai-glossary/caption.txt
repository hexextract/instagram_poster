Grounding. Deflection. Containment. Handoff. Here's what vendors mean, and what they're hoping you won't ask.

Eight terms, one honest translation each.

Grounding. Every answer is built from a fact retrieved out of your real store data. The only term here that changes whether an answer can be trusted. Always ask what, specifically, it is grounded in.

Deflection rate. The share of conversations that never reached a human. Translation: how many people gave up. Somebody who closed the tab in frustration is counted here as a success.

Containment. Deflection rate wearing a friendlier word. Same metric, same problem.

Handoff. Passing a conversation to a human. Ask whether the context travels with it. A handoff that makes the customer start again is not a handoff, it is a transfer.

Intent. What the customer wants, as opposed to the words they typed. Keyword bots match words, agents infer intent.

Hallucination. A confident answer with no source behind it. In ecommerce it is not a quirk, it is a promise you now own.

First response time. How long before the customer hears anything at all. The only speed number they personally experience.

True resolution. Solved, first time, no second message. The honest version of every other metric on this list.

Take these into your next demo call. When a vendor uses one, ask them to define it out loud. The definition you get back is more informative than the demo.
