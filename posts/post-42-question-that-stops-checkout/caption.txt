Most abandoned carts aren't indecision. They're an unanswered question.

Watch where it happens. Product page: convinced, adds to basket. Basket: still convinced, proceeds. Shipping step: a doubt appears. Nothing answers it. Tab closed, logged as an abandoned cart.

It is almost always one of three.

"Will it arrive in time?" They have an occasion. They need a date, not a range.

"Can I send it back?" They are managing the risk of being wrong.

"Is this the right size?" The single most common reason a full basket dies.

What most stores do next is send a discount three hours later. To somebody who was never hesitating about price. They wanted to know whether it arrives before the 14th, and the recovery email does not say.

You paid a margin point to not answer a question.

Recovery is expensive. Prevention is a sentence. One grounded reply, at the step, while the card is still on screen, drawn from your real shipping rules rather than a generic estimate.

That is the whole argument. Catch the doubt where it appears, not in an email three hours after the decision was already made.
