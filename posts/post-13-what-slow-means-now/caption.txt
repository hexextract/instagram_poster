"We reply within 24 hours" is not a reassurance. It's a warning.

The problem with your benchmark is that shoppers are not comparing you to other stores. They are comparing you to everything else in their phone, and you did not get a vote on that baseline.

A message to a friend: seconds. A food delivery update: live. A bank fraud alert: instant. Your support inbox: nine hours.

Against that, an hour does not read as busy or as a small team doing its best. It reads as indifference.

Read the line again too. "We reply within 24 hours" is a promise about your convenience, not theirs. Every service level you publish is really a sentence about whose time is worth more, and shoppers read it correctly.

One useful distinction, because it lets most stores off the hook more than they expect: first reply is not resolution. A complicated problem can take three days to sort out and the customer will stay perfectly calm, as long as the first reply came fast and told them somebody competent has it.

The speed that matters is the speed of the first response. That is the only one they personally experience.

Save this, then go and look at your actual first reply time. Not the average your helpdesk shows you. The median, including nights and weekends, which is when a lot of shopping happens.
