"Do you have this in navy?" is not a support ticket. It's a fitting room.

Half your inbox is people standing at the shelf.

In a shop, somebody holding a jumper and asking a question is the easiest sale of the day. You walk over, you answer, you mention the other colour. Online that exact person gets filed as a ticket and answered tomorrow, behind a damaged parcel from Tuesday.

What most systems do is answer and then stop. "The navy is out of stock at the moment." True, complete, and the sale is over, because nobody asks a second question after a dead end.

What a system that has actually read your catalogue does:

"The navy is out of stock, back around the 14th. The same cut in charcoal is in stock in your size, and the ink blue is the closest to navy we have right now. Want me to show you both?"

Three moves, and they are the same three a good shop assistant makes.

The alternative, because out of stock is only a dead end if nothing else is close.

The size up, because "runs small, most people size up" prevents a return and saves a sale at the same time.

The item that completes the set, which is only credible from something that has genuinely read the catalogue rather than guessed at it.

One line we will not cross: helpful, never pushy. A suggestion the shopper did not want is worse than no suggestion at all. It offers the alternative once, in the same message, and drops it the moment they move on.
