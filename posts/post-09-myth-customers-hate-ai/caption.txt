Customers don't hate AI. They hate being sent in a circle.

Three things founders believe, and what the behaviour actually shows.

"People want to talk to a human." People want the problem gone. A human is the route they trust because it is the route that has historically worked. Solve it in 20 seconds with software and almost nobody objects. Solve it in 20 hours with a person and everybody does.

"Automation feels impersonal." Nine hours of silence is the most impersonal thing your store does, and it is entirely human made. So is a macro pasted without reading the question.

"Bots make satisfaction scores drop." Satisfaction tracks whether the problem got solved, not who solved it. What drops the score is the loop, the handoff that loses context, and the third time somebody has to explain themselves from the start.

Here is what actually happened. A decade of decision trees wearing a chat bubble taught people that the chat icon is decorative. They learned the pattern, they stopped asking, and the bar moved so far down that anything which genuinely answers now feels remarkable.
