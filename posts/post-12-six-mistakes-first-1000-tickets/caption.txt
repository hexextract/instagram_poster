Nobody teaches you how to run support. So every store invents the same six mistakes.

None of this is a criticism. All six are what happens when support grows out of a founder's phone.

1. Nothing is written down. The returns window lives in your head, so it is thirty days on Monday and "we can probably stretch it" on Thursday. Your policy is a mood.

2. Nobody owns it. Support is the job everyone does a bit of, which means nobody is measured on it and nobody improves it.

3. Replying newest first. It feels productive because the top of the inbox clears. It means the person who has waited longest waits longest.

4. Treating every ticket as unique. They are not. Eight questions make up most of your volume and you have answered each of them a hundred times.

5. No plan for the other sixteen hours. Most stores have no after hours policy at all. Not a bad one. None.

6. Never reading tickets in aggregate. One at a time, every ticket is a chore. All of them at once, they are the best free product research you will ever get.

Fix six and most of the other five stop mattering, because you find out which ones are actually costing you.
