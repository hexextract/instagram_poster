Every great support reply has the same five parts. Most replies have two.

Here is the two part version, which is what most of them look like:

"Sorry about that! I have raised it with the courier and will update you when I hear back."

Polite, prompt, and the customer still has no idea what happens next or when.

The five parts, in this order, because the order matters:

1. Acknowledge. One line. Name the actual problem, not "your enquiry".

2. Answer directly. The answer goes second, never last.

3. Show the evidence. The tracking event, the policy line, the order date.

4. State what happens next. Who does what, by when.

5. Close the loop. Come back when it is done, unprompted.

The same reply, rebuilt:

"Your parcel was marked delivered at 11:40 on Tuesday to the front porch. That is a courier scan, not a signature, so it can be wrong. I have opened a missing parcel claim, reference 4471. The courier has 48 hours. If it is not found by Thursday I send a replacement that day, no further action from you."

Same facts. Five parts.

Two of these get skipped almost universally, and they are the two that build trust.

Answering second. The most common structural mistake is burying the answer under three sentences of apology. People scan for one thing and it should be in line two.

Showing evidence. "Your policy says 30 days" is an assertion. "You ordered on 3 March, which is day 34 of a 30 day window" is a shared fact. One invites an argument, the other ends it.

Never write "as per our policy". It reads as a door closing.

Save this and hold your next ten replies against it.
