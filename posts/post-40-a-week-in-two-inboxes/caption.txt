Same store. Same week. Two inboxes.

Inbox one: everything arrives with you. Sizing, stock, shipping, WISMO, returns, duties, one angry customer, and the one genuinely interesting question you never got to because it was seventh in the queue.

Inbox two: two escalations, both briefed, both worth your attention.

The important thing is what did not change. The questions did not go away. Same volume, same customers, same eight questions, and nobody was deflected or hidden from. They were answered, in about three seconds, at whatever hour they arrived.

What changed is the quality of what is left. Before: 212 things to read, sorted newest first, the angry one found at 5pm, nothing understood in aggregate. After: 17 things to read, each one briefed, the angry one first, the rest logged and tagged so you can see the pattern.

A queue became a shortlist.

The change founders actually mention first is not the hours. It is Sunday. Opening your phone on a Sunday evening stops being something you brace for.

One honest note, because the picture is easy to oversell. The right hand column is not empty, and it should not be. A store where nothing reaches a human is a store that has stopped listening to its customers. The goal was never zero.

Save this and picture your own week on the right. What would you do with the hours?
