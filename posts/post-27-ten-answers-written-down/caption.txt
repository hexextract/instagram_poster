If these ten answers aren't written down, you're improvising your policies daily.

Not badly. Just differently every time, depending on who replied and what kind of week it has been.

An unwritten policy is a mood. Thirty days on Monday, "we can probably stretch it" on Thursday, and then a customer quotes Thursday back at you and you have no ground to stand on.

The ten:

1. Shipping times, per region. A real date range, not "3 to 7 working days" with no start point.

2. What happens when it is late. At what point do you reship, and who decides.

3. International duties. Who pays, roughly how much, and where it says so at checkout.

4. The returns window. From order date or delivery date. Pick one.

5. The exchange process. Do they return first or do you ship first. This one is always vague.

6. Damage claims. What evidence you need, and what you do within how long.

7. Sizing guidance. Not the size chart. The sentence: "runs small, size up if between sizes."

8. Restock policy. Whether you restock, how you tell people, what you say when you will not.

9. Address changes and cancellations. The exact cut off, in hours, after which the answer is no.

10. Order edits. Add an item, change a size, combine two orders. Yes, no, or until when.

Write each one as four lines: the question in the customer's words, the answer in one sentence, the one edge case that always comes up, and who decides when it is not clear.

Forty lines. One afternoon. It outlasts every tool you will ever install.

And it is the only prep work automation ever needs, whatever you eventually use. Nothing can automate a policy you have not made.

Save this, it is your afternoon sorted.
