Eight questions. Forty times a day. That's a part time job, and you're the one doing it.

It is the same eight in almost every store. Will it fit. When will it arrive. Do you ship here. Is it back in stock. What is it made of. Can I return it. Can I change my address. Where is my order.

The obvious maths first. Forty a day, ninety seconds each to answer properly, six days a week. Six hours a week, just typing.

But the cost is not the ninety seconds. It is the interruption around it.

A question that takes ninety seconds to answer takes twenty minutes out of whatever you were doing before it arrived. You were doing something that needed a run up: product copy, a supplier call, next season's buy. The question arrives, you answer it because it is only ninety seconds, and you never get back to the same depth.

Repeat that forty times and the day never reaches full speed.

So the honest total is six hours of typing and roughly a day of lost depth. That second number never appears anywhere, because it happens to the work you did not do.

Which is the real point. You are not behind because you are disorganised. You are behind because the same eight questions arrive at random intervals from strangers, and every one of them genuinely deserves an answer.

Save this, then count your own eight.
